# Healthcare Commercial Strategy & Market Prioritization

## Executive Question
How should a healthcare commercial team allocate limited commercial resources across products, regions, physicians, and patient-continuation initiatives?

## Evidence Base
This case uses wholly synthetic, fictional commercial and episode data, not de-identified real-world patient records. The fixed-seed reference run reports 410.70 million simulated monetary units in revenue, 152.70 million in gross profit, a 37.18% gross margin, 97,624 aggregated new-patient events in commercial records (not unique patients), and a simulated 21.94% episode-discontinuation rate. None of these figures is evidence of actual business or clinical performance.

## Structured Diagnosis
### 1. Market attractiveness
Prioritize combinations of geography, therapy area, and product where patient volume and growth potential are high. Avoid using revenue alone because a high-revenue market may already be saturated.

### 2. Commercial performance
Evaluate revenue, gross profit, and margin together. A product with high revenue but weak margin should be treated differently from a lower-revenue product with stronger economics.

### 3. Physician engagement
Use physician potential and current engagement to identify "high-potential / low-engagement" accounts. These are candidates for focused commercial activity rather than blanket increases in sales calls.

### 4. Patient continuity
Track adherence and therapy discontinuation as a separate strategic lens. High acquisition with weak continuation can create a misleading view of performance.

### 5. Resource allocation
An optional **proposed, not yet implemented** decision score could weight the following illustrative considerations:
- 30% market attractiveness
- 30% profitability
- 20% physician whitespace
- 20% patient-continuity improvement potential

The score is a decision aid, not a substitute for clinical, regulatory, or field judgment.

## Recommendations
1. Protect high-profit, high-reach product-region combinations before expanding into weaker segments.
2. Target high-potential physicians with below-median engagement rather than increasing activity uniformly.
3. Investigate products or patient cohorts with above-average discontinuation before scaling acquisition.
4. Use a 90-day pilot in selected priority segments and track revenue per call, prescriptions per call, new patients, continuation, gross margin, and incremental commercial cost.
5. Reallocate resources only after comparing pilot uplift with a control or historical baseline.

## Risks and Caveats
- The dataset is synthetic, so findings are illustrative.
- Commercial performance does not establish clinical effectiveness.
- Patient-related recommendations must remain compliant with privacy, medical, and regulatory requirements.
- Correlation between commercial activity and outcomes should not be interpreted as causation without a stronger experimental design.
